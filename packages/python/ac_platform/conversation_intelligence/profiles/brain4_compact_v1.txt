SCORING RULES (all 18 dimensions)
- Score each dimension 1-5, or "-" (rating null). Use "-" when the behaviour cannot be observed: reason NEE (not enough evidence: recording starts late or ends early, no opportunity) or NA (not reasonably required for this call type). Never turn missing evidence into a low score; never give 5 for politeness on a near-empty call. Give 1 only for clearly observed damaging behaviour.
- 5 needs positive evidence that the skill improved the call. "Nothing went wrong" is 4.
- Judge the whole relevant pattern: frequency, severity, importance of the moment, recovery, effect on the buyer. One small miss can still be 4; one great moment cannot lift a weak pattern to 5.
- Confidence (high/medium/low) is separate and never changes the score.
- Never score: the outcome (bought or declined), call length, buyer friendliness, personality or charisma, accent, Hindi/Marathi/English mixing, grammar, "sir", script compliance, ritual phrases, or counts (questions, acknowledgements, talk ratio). A respected "no" or a genuine budget limit can be 5.
- Observations before interpretations; label inferences; never mind-read emotion or intent. Never call something a lie without evidence: an unverifiable claim is "verification needed" (ethics WARN), not FAIL.
- When one moment touches several dimensions, score each only for its own effect and name the shared evidence; do not copy the same criticism into two dimensions.
- Identify the call type first (inbound, cold, scheduled, follow-up, referral, existing client, re-engagement, operational): what is needed depends on it.
- Coaching must name the observed behaviour in this call and give a concrete cue ("On your next relevant call, ..."). Never vague ("close harder", "explain better") and never identity labels ("you are a bad listener").

CATEGORY 1 - HOW YOU TALK
D01 Starting the Call. The opening until the call moves into plan, discovery or substance. Look for: natural (not robotic) entry; context awareness; low resistance; adapting to immediate signals (busy, driving); no premature pitching; smooth move into substance. Not required: "how are you", name, small talk. Not here: agenda (D02), whole-call delivery (D03), rapport over the call (D06), ethics (D07) unless deception wrecks the entry.
5 natural, context-aware, helps; a minimal opener can be 5 if nothing more was needed. 4 worked; slightly formal, generic, abrupt or brief harmless small talk. 3 transactional, form-filling, scripted, mild talk-over then recovery, slightly unprepared. 2 pitch before the buyer speaks; ignoring "I'm driving"; forced small talk; unprepared ("you filled something somewhere"). 1 aggressive "just listen"; overriding "can't talk"; deceptive "not a sales call"; hostile or condescending. "-" opening missing or corrupted.

D02 Setting the Plan. Orientation around the move into discovery; the word "agenda" is not needed. Look for: why-this-call clarity; useful direction ("understand first, then show only what's relevant"); a reason for asking questions; appropriate participation; realistic expectations; the buyer's own priorities included; natural transition. A follow-up recap of pending items can be 5; a short operational call needs none ("-" NA). Credit adopting the buyer's agenda, short frames, briefly answering an early "what does it do?", time framing when the buyer is short of time. Penalise frames that pre-commit to a sale, submissive over-permission, controlling frames ("our process, just answer").
5 clear, buyer-friendly, flexible frame that creates alignment (incl. explaining why brief discovery comes before the price a buyer asked for). 4 functional but generic or slightly seller-led. 3 minimal ("a few questions first"), seller-centred, scripted, ignores the buyer's stated priority. 2 questions with no reason; rigid agenda against the buyer's time limit; hidden discovery after a price request; needed framing and got none. 1 deceptive purpose; trapping yes-or-no-today frame; "no sir, this is our process" after the buyer rejects the plan.

D03 Tone, Pace & Pauses. Needs reliable audio. With a transcript only, rate "-" (NEE) unless the transcript itself shows clear repeated delivery problems (constant talk-over, rapid stacked questions, answering own questions, monologues): then score with low confidence. Attention: sensitive questions, disclosures, explanation, price, the first seconds after an objection, the close. Positive: pause after important questions, price and disclosures; slowing down at objections; plain price without apology; letting silence work after a close. Negative: rapid-fire stacked questions; speed-up after objections; filling every silence; nervous or apologetic price; answering own question; "no pressure though..." after a close question. Inattentive interruptions belong to D04. Never reward a "sales voice" or penalise a quiet natural voice.
5 consistent, adaptive, calm under pressure in several parts. 4 one rushed section. 3 consistently slightly fast or flat, fills some silences. 2 repeated friction (stacked questions, panic after objection, constant filling, nervous price). 1 raised voice, continuous domination, mocking.

D04 Listening Well. Test: does what the salesperson says next prove they understood what came before? Whole call incl. price, objections, close. Evidence: tracks facts (numbers, team size, dates, stakeholders); responds to meaning; follow-ups built on answers; recalls earlier statements; clarifies contradictions without weaponising them; lets the buyer finish; no re-asking answered questions; keeps the buyer's own words. Not evidence: silence, talk ratio, acknowledgement words. Severity: misremembering money, commitments or authority is material; ignoring a key disclosure or authority statement is material; quick self-correction is fine.
5 consistent tracking, recall, contradiction handling. 4 one repeated question, one interruption with "sorry, continue", one secondary thread missed. 3 scripted jumps, a couple of repeats, "I understand" without proof, finishing the buyer's sentences, a missed contradiction. 2 script over answers, repeated re-asking, assuming the ending, answering a concern the buyer said wasn't theirs. 1 buyer says "you're not listening"; repeated domination; a material fact or critical disclosure ignored.

D05 Asking the Right Questions. Test: was each important question the next useful one? Never count questions. Evidence: relevance; clarifying vague terms; follow-up depth (surface -> context -> cause -> impact); testing hypotheses; noticing missing information; well-framed difficult questions; logical sequence; questions that make the buyer think; stopping when clear. What comes after an unexpected answer reveals most; buying signals should move questioning to decision readiness. Anti-patterns: interview checklists, leading questions, manufacturing pain, assuming impact, hiding direct answers behind more questions, endless discovery. Do not penalise skipping what is already known.
5 consistently adaptive and deepening, knows when to stop. 4 one missed thread, generic openers then sharp follow-ups, one premature budget question. 3 mostly surface, checklist mix, accepts vague answers. 2 checklist with no follow-up, ignores an important answer, leading, premature diagnosis. 1 aggressive interrogation, manufacturing pain against denial, manipulation.

D06 Building Connection. Test: did the buyer have reason to feel safe enough to be honest (disagree, doubt, admit failure, say no)? The real test is after the easy part: disagreement, price, objection, "no". Not rewarded: personality, small talk, compliments, shared hometown. Trust is observed in behaviour (voluntary disclosure of failures, money, fears, open disagreement). Signals: genuine interest; prospect-side thinking; non-judgement; meaningful acknowledgement; safe disagreement; no personality shift at money or close. Vulnerability sequence: acknowledgement -> space -> understanding is strong; -> pitch is weak (about 2); -> pressure is damaging (1). Approval-seeking (agreeing with everything, discounting, constant apology) is not rapport.
5 strong behavioural evidence that the relationship enabled honesty. 4 slightly formal but genuine; a mild defensive moment repaired. 3 polite but transactional; repetitive "I understand"; slight shift at objection. 2 fake rapport; judgemental remarks; empathy used as a pitch bridge; personality shift at price. 1 humiliation; exploiting vulnerability; using a private disclosure as leverage.

D07 Being Professional + Ethics Gate (PASS / WARN / FAIL). One material violation = 1 and FAIL even after a good call; FAIL is never averaged away. Hard violations: deliberate lie, fake urgency or scarcity, fabricated proof, unsupported guarantee as fact, hiding material information, knowingly selling a misfit, pressure after a clear no, false competitor claims, invented ROI, misrepresenting commercial terms, ignoring stop. Unverifiable claims: WARN with "verification needed". Weigh misstated commercial terms heavily (price, discount, refund, guarantee, EMI, start date, deliverables). Professional is not formal or passive: direct asks and strong recommendations are fine. Credit: "I don't know, I'll confirm"; correcting own errors; declining a wrong-fit sale; one clarifying question after a no, then respect.
5 PASS consistently truthful, separates certainty levels, respects no. 4 one overconfident claim self-corrected. 3 casual competitor criticism, mild overstatement later qualified, repeated "why wait?". 2 escalating pressure, repeated unsupported competitor attacks (WARN; FAIL if material). 1 FAIL: fake deadline (only if shown false), unsupported guarantee, pressure after "no, please stop", knowingly selling a misfit.

CATEGORY 2 - HOW WELL YOU UNDERSTAND THE BUYER
D08 Finding the Decision-Makers. Is there an accurate authority map: who approves, who influences, who is only informed, shared or split authority, who is missing; does the call move towards involving the right people? HOW and WHEN they decide is D12. Do not assume the person on the call, a senior title or a spouse mention means anything until diagnosed. Stated joint-decision rules are legitimate: the right move is a joint conversation, never bypass. Reconfirm authority when price or scope changes. Keep it proportional (no long qualification for simple consumer sales).
5 roles clarified, shared rules respected with a joint call. 4 right stakeholder but role thin, or identified late with good recovery. 3 "I'll discuss with my wife" -> "okay"; authority surfaced only at the close; influencer treated as approver. 2 a stated co-decider ignored and the wrong person closed; spouse dismissed as an excuse. 1 encourages secret payment or keeping the decision-maker out. "-" early exploratory call or operational call.

D09 Understanding Problems & Goals. By the end, can the salesperson state current state -> main problem -> likely causes or open uncertainty -> desired state -> gap, in the buyer's terms? Impact is D11; current approach and alternatives are D10. Reward moving from vague to specific, separating symptom from cause, primary vs secondary problems, eliciting the buyer's own goal, prioritising goals, confirming with a summary. Penalise accepting "sales are down" as the diagnosis, invented causes, solution-first reframing, seller-imposed goals, manufactured pain, presenting before problem and goal are clear.
5 full model plus confirmation, uncertainty stated, goals prioritised. 4 goal slightly vague or cause not pinned. 3 surface only, many issues without priority. 2 vague problem accepted and pitched; invented cause; goal never asked; steering to the seller's preferred problem. 1 pushes an exaggerated or false problem against denial.

D10 Understanding What They're Doing Now. Does the salesperson hold a model of (a) the current approach (process, tools, what works, where it breaks), (b) previous attempts (what was tried, what happened, why stopped; failure cause kept as a hypothesis), (c) alternatives (competitors, in-house, do nothing) and why the buyer likes them? Use the latest call's evidence when the setup has changed. Penalise recommending what was already tried, dismissing current tools or competitors without understanding them, premature replacement when complementing fits.
5 clear current system and gap, a past attempt diagnosed, competitor appeal understood without attack. 4 one past attempt or competitor motive shallow. 3 tool name only; "agency failed" accepted; competitor noted not explored. 2 recommends what was already tried; dismisses a tool unexamined. 1 keeps pitching replacement of something that works; invents competitor weaknesses.

D11 Understanding the Real Impact. Did the salesperson establish how much the problem matters (financial, time, effort, emotional, opportunity, cost of inaction, desired upside) without manufacturing pain? Tag numbers by source (buyer-stated / calculated / seller estimate / unsupported). One impact type can suffice.
5 grounded picture of the impact that matters to this buyer, quantified where reality allows. 4 main impact clear, cost of inaction or upside missing. 3 vague ("losing money" accepted) or ungrounded guess. 2 clear impact signal ignored; jumped to the solution before consequences; number forced on the buyer. 1 invented loss, guaranteed ROI, exploited personal pain. "-" recording starts after discovery, operational call.

D12 Understanding How They Will Decide. Process steps and owners, criteria (priorities, must-haves, disqualifiers), remaining uncertainty, timing or trigger with a reason; separate decided-but-payment-pending from not-decided. Who decides is D08; the agreed next action is D18.
5 can explain what must happen, what matters, what is unresolved and when. 4 one or two minor gaps. 3 "need to think" met with timing only; vague future date. 2 accepts "I'll think" without diagnosis; reduces criteria to price without evidence. 1 overrides a legitimate process (pay before the co-decider's review; fake deadline). "-" early discovery or recording ends before decision talk.

CATEGORY 3 - HOW WELL YOU MOVE THE SALE FORWARD
D13 Matching the Right Solution. Did the recommendation fit the diagnosed problem, goal, impact, past attempts, current setup, decision criteria, capacity and realistic expectations? Map each recommended capability to buyer evidence. A smaller plan, a complement or no sale can be 5.
5 specific, prioritised, buyer-grounded, honest. 4 right with minor prioritisation gaps. 3 broadly right but generic; feature list with a few relevant items. 2 premature prescription; mismatched to the main gap; upsell-driven. 1 clearly wrong or concealing a limit. "-" discovery-only call or recording ends before a recommendation.

D14 Explaining the Solution Clearly. Could a normal buyer leave with an accurate mental model: what it does, how it works, why it matters to them, who does what, scope, limits, realistic outcome? Buyer-specific language, mechanism not magic, top relevant points first, checking understanding, stopping when clear. Do not reward length or feature count.
5 buyer-specific, mechanism shown, understanding checked, adapts. 4 minor jargon or one unneeded feature. 3 components without mechanism, overlong tour with comprehension unmanaged. 2 feature dump; material implementation burden omitted. 1 materially false capability or guaranteed outcome. "-" no meaningful explanation.

D15 Talking About Price & Budget. Budget context, price stated clearly and calmly, pause, then the right question; classify the money state (cannot afford / not allocated / expected lower / doesn't see value / cash-flow timing / negotiation / logistics). Record the price exactly.
5 right timing, no apology or panic, lets silence work, separates affordability from value, transparent options. 4 slight hesitation or over-explained options. 3 generic "budget?" accepted; defends price before any reaction; unprompted discount. 2 successive discounts into silence; EMI aimed at a value doubt; shaming. 1 fake anchor, fake discount or deadline, hidden fees (ethics FAIL). "-" no price discussion.

D16 Handling Objections. When the buyer resisted: hear, pause, acknowledge, clarify, isolate, diagnose with call evidence, resolve the actual blocker truthfully, re-check, return to decision only after the state changes. Record stated objection -> possible meanings -> root blocker (confirmed / likely / unknown); type (financial, value, risk, trust, timing, authority, fit, implementation, emotional, comparative, avoidance). A question, disagreement or silence is not an objection.
5 precise diagnosis, targeted resolution, re-check; a true no respected. 4 correct core, weak re-check. 3 partial diagnosis; surface resolved with a deeper blocker unchecked. 2 instant rebuttal, feature dump, arguing, re-closing without new information. 1 fake scarcity, exploiting fear, ignoring a repeated no. "-" no meaningful objection (never 5 just because nothing went wrong).

D17 Asking for the Decision. Readiness (problem, goal, fit, authority, commercial clarity, objections, buying signals; clusters beat single signals), then the clearest stage-appropriate ask (purchase, stakeholder meeting, validation, start date, or no sale), then silence. The buyer self-closing and the seller moving operationally is 5; a clear no can be 5.
5 recognises readiness, stops selling, asks directly, lets the buyer think. 4 slightly delayed or indirect. 3 vague ask, readiness recognised late, talking through the pause. 2 avoids the ask despite clear signals ("I'll send a brochure"), repeated premature asks, talking past the yes. 1 shame close, fake urgency, closing around the decision-maker, pushing after a clear no. "-" discovery-only or recording ends early.

D18 Setting Clear Next Steps. Convert the final state (yes / no / deferred / stakeholder, information, commercial or timing pending / nurture / no fit) into action, owner, timing or trigger, purpose and expected output; buyer agreed. Keep proposed vs agreed separate; seller vs buyer actions separate.
5 specific, owned, timed or event-anchored, purposeful, agreed; clean closure after a no. 4 trigger clear, no time or deliverable named. 3 date without purpose; "send details" undefined; seller-side action only. 2 "I'll follow up" / "let me know" despite a real event; yes with no payment or onboarding path. 1 ignores requested space; fake urgency; chasing after "don't follow up". "-" recording ends early.
