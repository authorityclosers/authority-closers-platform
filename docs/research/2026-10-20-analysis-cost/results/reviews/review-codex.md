Verdict: **disagree** — the study supports a promising research candidate, but does not establish “≤ ₹0.40 tomorrow without losing quality” or a validated ₹0.10 pipeline.

1. **Major — the arithmetic works, but two different cost models are presented as “today.”** From the staging token fits and prices (`paper/01-cost-anatomy.md:18–26`, `paper/costmodel.py:22–24,64–69`), fixed cost is `100 × (7,142×0.75 + 11,838×3.75)/1e6 = ₹4.9749`; variable cost including Scribe is `100 × (2,978×0.75 + 626×3.75)/1e6 + 100×0.22/60 = ₹0.82477/min`. Thus today is **₹1.9303 / ₹1.1853 / ₹0.9077 per minute** at 4.5 / 13.8 / 60 minutes, matching section 1 and `costmodel.py`. Independently refitting the ledger using the harness's last-success rule instead gives `₹6.3664 + ₹0.19814×minutes` for analysis: with Scribe, **₹1.980 / ₹1.026 / ₹0.671**, matching section 6, but materially different, especially on long calls. The recommendation uses the latter baseline without reconciling its much smaller variable cost. **Fix:** retain separate production-fit and synthetic-benchmark baselines, explain the discrepancy, and validate savings against the actual duration/language mix; do not substitute benchmark “today” for production “today.”

2. **Major — “measured Flex” is a projection, with short-call and fallback exceptions.** The saved fits are ASR `₹0.0896 + ₹0.1375m` and oracle v2 analysis `₹1.9903 + ₹0.0342m` (`results/scores/fits.json`, corresponding model/arm rows). Their sum is `₹2.0799/m + ₹0.1717/min`, or **₹0.634 / ₹0.322 / ₹0.206**; halving gives **₹0.317 / ₹0.161 / ₹0.103**. These reproduce the paper, but `harness/fits.py:67–82` simply multiplies standard costs by 0.5 and uses oracle analysis rather than the available ASR-input fit. Neither provider sender requests Flex (`harness/providers.py:56–59,84–107`). For fx-07, actual paired receipts total $0.024817 over 2.4967 minutes (`results/ledger.jsonl:178,883`; `bench/audio/fx-07/render.json`), hence **₹0.497/min even after halving**. Standard fallback also breaks the short-call target (`paper/07-recommendation.md:32`). **Fix:** label discounts as estimates; measure the native paid-provider Flex route, full latency including retries, fallback frequency, and cost over the real call mix. Define whether the target is aggregate or per call. The rounded ₹0.10 at 60 minutes is ₹0.103, above a strict ceiling.

3. **Blocker — the recommended arm fails the study's own quality gates.** In `results/scores/facts.md`, compare the Haiku-judged rows for `onepass2/3.8 Flash/3.1-FL-ASR` and `today/3.8 Flash/oracle`: recall **71.1% vs 70.6%**, critical **76.8% vs 74.6%**, but precision **94.8% vs 99.7%**, hallucination **5.2% vs 0.3%**, and needles **70.8% vs 75.0%**. Precision and hallucination fail M2/M4; needle recall fails M6's ≥90% requirement (`paper/03-benchmark-design.md:13–18`). Scribe was never measured (`paper/06-results.md:35–36`), so the claim that today's ASR “pays the same price” is unsupported (`:67–70`). **Fix:** describe the candidate as failing current acceptance gates. An owner-authorized paired Scribe-versus-Gemini test with independently annotated real calls could flip the recommendation if critical errors, attribution, unsupported claims and coaching meet the declared gates; price or aggregate recall alone cannot.

4. **Major — point estimates do not establish non-inferiority.** The design requires 95% call-bootstrap bounds and three runs (`paper/03-benchmark-design.md:22,27–30`); the tables provide neither bounds nor complete three-run comparisons. Only **16 calls and 24 needles** underlie the principal comparison (`results/scores/facts.json`, primary-judge per-call rows). My paired percentile bootstrap, resampling 16 calls 20,000 times with seed 20261020 and recomputing pooled ratios, gives candidate-minus-today intervals of **−7.0 to +8.5 percentage points recall**, **−5.5 to +10.1 critical recall**, and **+2.7 to +7.3 hallucination**. Even this imperfect comparator fails the prescribed lower-bound tests. **Fix:** publish paired intervals, language/depth results and run variability, then evaluate a held-out, adequately sized set after freezing prompts. Facts within a call are not independent samples.

5. **Major — the scoring favours the new extraction contract and cannot verify several claims.** Candidate fact types deliberately match the benchmark vocabulary (`harness/shapes.py:1–14`), and v2 was tuned for exhaustive facts (`:97–104`; `paper/06-results.md:51–53`). Today's fact metric excludes facts in C5 and pointer outputs (`harness/today.py:122–130`), although their costs are included. Both arms' judge inputs discard speaker roles, normalized fields and segment anchors (`harness/run.py:278–302`), so this is not an exact-match or attribution evaluation. Gemini judges Gemini and Haiku judges Haiku; all report rows use Haiku alone (`results/scores/report.md`), despite the two-judge claim (`paper/06-results.md:3–4`). Two different judge families do not prevent self-family bias. Their Luna needle estimates differ by **8.4 points**, exceeding the claimed 1–5-point agreement (`results/scores/facts.md`, Luna oracle rows). **Fix:** compare equivalent user-visible facts with a common lossless adapter; validate roles, amounts and evidence separately; use independent blinded report judges and human adjudication, and hold out calls by writer as well as language. The current judges' errors are uncalibrated.

6. **Major — speaker “accuracy” is not a valid diarization or role-recovery gate.** `harness/score.py:121–130` assigns each hypothesis segment to the nearest reference turn using its midpoint, then independently maps each hypothesis speaker to its majority reference speaker. This permits many hypothesis speakers to map to one true speaker, ignores missed speech and within-segment speaker changes, and trusts potentially drifting timestamps. A distinct speaker label per turn could score perfectly. There is no scored evidence for “the analysis pass ... recovers most” role errors (`paper/07-recommendation.md:24–25`). **Fix:** use one-to-one speaker mapping with DER/cpWER/WDER, assess timestamp drift and overlap, and score the final attributed commitments/objections against reference roles.

7. **Major — audio evidence does not transfer cleanly to real sales calls, and cohorts differ.** Marathi TTS uses Hindi voices and was selected using Whisper round-trip checks (`paper/05-method.md:39–48`), introducing both pronunciation distortion and possible selection bias. FLEURS supplies read sentences, not spontaneous code-mixed multi-party sales conversations (`paper/06-results.md:10–20`). Quality denominators differ: Gemini 3.8 retention has **477** critical facts, 3.1 has **535**, and Groq **489** (`results/scores/retain.md`, named rows). ASR scoring also includes every JSON call, including public-speech bundles (`harness/score.py:97–105`), and omits `.failed` files written by `harness/run.py:204–207`. Consequently, unconditional rankings and “fine on calls” are too strong. **Fix:** freeze a common paired cohort, count all attempted calls and failures, separate public speech from fictional calls, manually check rendered critical words, and test real noisy code-mixed calls through approved providers.

8. **Major — ledger-derived costs omit attempts and mix billing conventions.** Both cost paths keep only the last successful request for a call/tag (`harness/score.py:278–290`; `harness/fits.py:27–36`). For example, both C4 receipts at `results/ledger.jsonl:70,82` incurred cost, but only the latter survives. Research reruns may reasonably be excluded, but the ledger does not distinguish them from billable repair/recovery attempts. Internal HTTP retries are not individually logged (`harness/common.py:94–117`). Moreover, OpenRouter costs prefer billed amounts while the table says “list”; its audio `list_usd` calculation ignores audio-specific input pricing (`harness/providers.py:109–124`). Row 139 records **$0.003561 list vs $0.004804 billed**. **Fix:** identify logical runs and every attempt, publish successful-run and operational costs separately, include billable retries/repairs, and reconcile modality-aware list prices with receipts. Do not run the scoring scripts unchanged during review: they write results.

9. **Blocker — the replacement's product/scoring contract is unproven.** The study correctly requires owner approval for production and for Google's new audio flow (`paper/07-recommendation.md:27–34`; `paper/08-next-steps.md:3–10`). However, “stop now, whatever else” proposes removing outputs (`paper/07-recommendation.md:52–56`), while the replacement generates fresh 0–100 pointer scores (`harness/shapes.py:51,75–79`) and maps them into existing stores. That is insufficient to demonstrate the brief's **AC-SVAL: no official scoring changes** constraint. Parsing JSON is also not schema/product validation (`harness/run.py:234–238`); pointer/dimension arrays do not enforce complete unique membership (`harness/shapes.py:75–90`). **Fix:** keep experimental scores in research/shadow outputs, preserve official scoring, validate the full report/store contract and downstream consumers, and obtain explicit owner approval for any production change or output removal. Existing Google transcript use does not authorize audio or an OpenRouter intermediary for real data.

10. **Major — ₹0.10 is economically plausible only under unresolved conditions.** `paper/07-recommendation.md:44` quotes Luna's oracle quality, while its end-to-end quality was still “judging” (`paper/06-results.md:63`). Even the oracle needle result changes from 91.7% to 83.3% on run 2 (`results/scores/facts.md`, Luna/Haiku rows). `harness/fits.py:80–83` assumes a further 50% Luna discount; the proposed 4.5-minute pipeline still costs **₹0.12/min**, and the additional coaching pass is only estimated. Self-hosting lacks full hardware, utilization, diarization, operations and repair costs; using Luna still sends data outside AC, contrary to the self-hosted row's “no data leaves AC” wording (`paper/07-recommendation.md:47`). **Fix:** require end-to-end real-call quality, measured discounted pricing/availability, duration-mix costs and all-in hosting costs. Gate the exact provider/host, region/residency, retention, training use and DPA before real data. Coding an OpenAI route or naming an India host establishes none of these.

11. **Major — test cheaper changes that preserve the existing contract first.** The evidence already identifies large fixed outputs, zero cache hits, repeated transcript reads, a periodic pointer timer and untrimmed audio (`paper/01-cost-anatomy.md:28,71–77,84–88`). Yet the main recommendation changes ASR, extraction, scoring output and orchestration together. **Fix:** benchmark idempotency/content-hash reuse and timer no-op suppression; compact repeated JSON fields and generate deterministic report scaffolding locally; share validated intermediate facts; measure actual caching and batch/Flex availability; and investigate silence/hold trimming with a reversible timestamp map. Evaluate each change through current approved providers with the official scoring contract preserved. Cache savings affect input spend only, whereas output tokens dominate; do not assume caching alone reaches the target.
