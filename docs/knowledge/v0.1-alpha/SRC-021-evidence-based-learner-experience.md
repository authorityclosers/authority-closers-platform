# Evidence-based learner experience research

This node records the bounded research interpretation used for the v0.1 learner redesign. Controlled AC documents remain authoritative. External sources can support interaction and learning-mechanism decisions but cannot create new business semantics, access rules or canonical progress.

Evidence sources include [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [W3C Accessible Authentication](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html), [Nielsen usability heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/), [Roediger and Karpicke on retrieval practice](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/), [Mayer on multimedia learning](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jcal.12197), and [Guo, Kim and Rubin on MOOC video engagement](https://eddatax.fed.cuhk.edu.hk/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/How-Video-Production-Affects-Student-Engagement-An-Empirical-Study-of-MOOC-Videos.pdf).

Product references include official Udemy, Coursera, Open edX, Duolingo and Graphy documentation. The [Unacademy redesign case study](https://thehardcopy.co/unacademy-rewrites-the-book/) and user-provided screenshots are explicitly case-study/visual evidence, not causal proof.

Applied inferences: one dominant next action; user-based navigation containing only working v0.1 routes; in-app free enrollment; a media-first course stage with persistent outline; a visible Watch → Reflect → Implement → Review → Improve loop; mobile-native navigation; honest unavailable states; and one tokenized light/dark/state system.
