## Selected experience

Open directly to a compact, responsive Overview. Show the caller, measured call
duration, evidenced outcome and one-line diagnosis, then these fourteen parts:

1. What you already do well: up to three specific strengths and why they matter.
2. The most critical improvement: what happened, evidence, consequence and replacement behavior.
3. The second critical improvement with the same short structure.
4. The third important improvement.
5. Two or three golden moments worth repeating.
6. Realistic missed opportunities, with what the prospect said and the missed follow-up.
7. Prospect words versus possible underlying concern; distinguish inference from evidence.
8. A short replay list with precise source-bound clips and useful priority labels.
9. The first meaningful conversation breakpoint: before, change, after and the supported causal explanation.
10. Eight-factor skills and separate ethics assessment, with evidence and uncertainty.
11. One next-call behavior and a measurable target.
12. One practice drill tied to that behavior.
13. Progress only when comparable previous calls exist; do not manufacture a first-call trend.
14. A short final verdict covering strengths, biggest problem and the one next step.

All fourteen parts are part of the free overview when supporting data exists.
Progress needs prior authorized calls; omission for a first call is a data state,
not a sales lock. Other tabs can expand evidence, playback and deeper analysis.
The shared account provides saved-call continuity; a fixed 70/30 byte split is
not an acceptance criterion. App controls remain simple English; report and
transcript content preserve the original mixed language and script.

Free access belongs to the current private guest owner. It does not publish
recordings or report URLs to other visitors. Source ownership, retention,
server-side processing allowances and consent continue to apply.

The template's example timestamps, XX scores, caller names and business numbers
must not be displayed as results. Financial estimates need actual input data,
named assumptions and a transparent calculation; otherwise show insufficient
data. The listed factor weights total 95 while the template states 100. Preserve
the existing SVAL hold on numeric publication rather than inventing a correction.
This does not prevent the evidence-backed qualitative overview from shipping.
